DIRECT & INDIRECT SPEECH

CUET UG English Grammar  |  Quick-recap notes with flowcharts

Reporting words

+ tense back-shift

Direct speech gives the exact words in quotes. Indirect speech reports them without quotes, usually with a tense shift, pronoun change and time or place change.

1

Making Indirect Speech

Tense, pronoun and word changes

Start here: Convert to indirect (follow the arrows)

The reporting verb decides if the tense moves.

1.1  Tense back-shift

PRESENT SIMPLE

→ past simple

"I eat" → he said he ate.

PRESENT CONTINUOUS

→ past continuous

"I am eating" → he said he was eating.

PRESENT PERFECT

→ past perfect

"I have eaten" → he said he had eaten.

PAST SIMPLE

→ past perfect

"I ate" → he said he had eaten.

WILL / CAN / MAY

→ would / could / might

"I will go" → he said he would go.

PAST PERFECT

no change

"I had eaten" → he said he had eaten.

1.2  Word changes

NOW

→ then

TODAY

→ that day

TOMORROW

→ the next day

YESTERDAY

→ the day before

HERE

→ there

THIS

→ that

AGO

→ before

LAST NIGHT

→ the night before

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     P N T

Change three things: Pronoun, Now-words (time and place), Tense. He said, "I am here now." → He said that he was there then.

2

Questions, Commands and Exclamations

Each sentence type has its own link word

2.1  Which reporting verb?  (follow the arrows)

Question = if / whether. Command = to. Statement = that.

2.2  Rules that win marks

WH- QUESTIONS

keep the wh- word, drop the question order

"Where do you live?" → He asked me where I lived.

COMMANDS

told / ordered + to

"Sit down." → He told me to sit down.

REQUESTS

requested / begged + to

"Please help." → He requested me to help.

ADVICE

advised + to

"You should rest." → He advised me to rest.

NEGATIVE COMMAND

told + not to

"Do not run." → He told me not to run.

LET'S

suggested + -ing

"Let's go." → He suggested going.

WISHES

wished + noun

"Good luck!" → He wished me good luck.

GREETINGS

wished / greeted

"Good morning" → He wished me good morning.

THANKS

thanked

"Thank you" → He thanked me.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

No question mark and no inverted order in indirect speech: He asked where did I live → He asked where I lived.

 Say has no object; tell needs one: He told that → He told me that / He said that.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     S A Y   T O   T E L L ,   A S K   T O   A S K

"Say to" becomes told (statement) or asked (question). He said to me, "Go." → He told me to go.

3.3  Mixed practice: convert to indirect speech

1.  He said, "I am ill." →  He said that he ___ ill.

2.  She said, "I have finished." →  She said that she ___ finished.

3.  He asked, "Where do you live?" →  He asked me where I ___.

4.  He said to me, "Go home." →  He ___ me to go home.

5.  She said, "I will come tomorrow." →  She said that she would come ___.

6.  He asked, "Are you tired?" →  He asked me ___ I was tired.

7.  The teacher said, "The earth moves." →  The teacher said that the earth ___.

8.  He said, "Do not talk." →  He told me ___ to talk.

9.  She said, "I saw him yesterday." →  She said she ___ him the day before.

10.  He said, "Let us go." →  He ___ going.

Answers: 1 was, 2 had, 3 lived, 4 told, 5 the next day, 6 if / whether, 7 moves, 8 not, 9 had seen, 10 suggested.

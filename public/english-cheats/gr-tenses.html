1.1  The twelve tenses at a glance

PRESENT SIMPLE

V1 (+s/es)

She writes daily.

PRESENT CONTINUOUS

is/am/are + V-ing

She is writing now.

PRESENT PERFECT

has/have + V3

She has written it.

PRESENT PERFECT CONT.

has/have been + V-ing

She has been writing for an hour.

PAST SIMPLE

V2

She wrote yesterday.

PAST CONTINUOUS

was/were + V-ing

She was writing when I called.

PAST PERFECT

had + V3

She had written before I came.

PAST PERFECT CONT.

had been + V-ing

She had been writing for an hour.

FUTURE SIMPLE

will + V1

She will write tomorrow.

FUTURE CONTINUOUS

will be + V-ing

She will be writing at 5.

FUTURE PERFECT

will have + V3

She will have written by 5.

FUTURE PERFECT CONT.

will have been + V-ing

She will have been writing for an hour by 5.

1.2  Time words tell you the tense

SIMPLE PRESENT

always, usually, often, never, every day, on Sundays

PRESENT PERFECT

already, yet, just, ever, never, so far, recently, lately

PRESENT CONTINUOUS

now, at the moment, today, currently, look!, listen!

PAST SIMPLE

yesterday, last week, ago, in 2010, once, then

PAST CONTINUOUS

while, when (during an action), at that time, all day

PAST PERFECT

before, after, by the time, already (in the past), had...when

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     S I N C E   &   F O R

Since = point, For = period. Since 2020, since Monday, since I was ten. / For six years, for two hours, for a long time. Both take the perfect tense: I have lived here since 2020 / for six years.

2.2  Present perfect, past simple and special patterns

PRESENT PERFECT

result matters now or time is unfinished

I have finished my work. She has lived here for years.

PAST SIMPLE

finished, named time

I finished it yesterday. We met in 2019.

UNIVERSAL TRUTH

always present simple

Water boils at 100°C.

HARDLY / NO SOONER

past perfect first

Hardly had he left when it rained. No sooner had she come than...

WISH / AS IF / IT IS TIME

followed by the past

It is time we left. I wish I knew. He talks as if he were king.

SINCE CLAUSE

past in since-clause, perfect in main

It has been five years since we met.

USED TO + V1

past habit, not now

I used to walk daily.

SEQUENCE OF TENSES

past main verb → past second verb

He said that he was ill.

TIMETABLES

present simple for the future

The train leaves at six.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Never use the present perfect with a finished-time word: I have seen him yesterday → I saw him yesterday.

 No will after if / when: if it will rain → if it rains.

 No -ing for stative verbs: I am knowing → I know.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     P - E - R - F - E - C - T

Perfect tense = have/had/will have + V3. Ask: "Is the action complete, with a link to another time?" If yes, perfect. If it is just a finished event with a named time, use the simple past.

3.3  Mixed practice: choose the right tense

1.  I (have lived / lived) here since 2015.

2.  She (went / has gone) to Paris last year.

3.  When we reached, the film (started / had started).

4.  I will call you when he (will arrive / arrives).

5.  He (is knowing / knows) the answer.

6.  Hardly (had / has) he left when it rained.

7.  If you (will study / study) hard, you will pass.

8.  The sun (rises / rose) in the east.

9.  I (am waiting / have been waiting) for two hours.

10.  She (has finished / finished) the report yesterday.

Answers: 1 have lived, 2 went, 3 had started, 4 arrives, 5 knows, 6 had, 7 study, 8 rises, 9 have been waiting, 10 finished.

6 0 - S E C O N D   R E C A P

Time word first: yesterday/ago → past simple; since/for/already/yet → present perfect; now → continuous.

 Since = point, for = period.

 Earlier of two past actions → had + V3.

 After if / when / before → present for the future.

 know, like, want never take -ing.

 Hardly / no sooner → had + subject + V3.

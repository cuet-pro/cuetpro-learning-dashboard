QUESTION TAGS

CUET UG English Grammar  |  Quick-recap notes with flowcharts

Isn't it?

+ opposite rule

A question tag is a short question added to a statement ("...isn't it?"). The rule is simple: positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag, using the same helping verb and a pronoun.

1

The Opposite Rule

Build a tag in three steps

Start here: Build the tag (follow the arrows)

Opposite sign, same helping verb, pronoun at the end.

1.1  Three steps

STEP 1  FLIP THE SIGN

positive → negative; negative → positive

He is here, isn't he?

STEP 2  SAME VERB

repeat the helping verb

She has gone, hasn't she?

STEP 3  USE A PRONOUN

never a noun

Ram is kind, isn't he?

1.2  Examples by tense

PRESENT SIMPLE

do / does

You like tea, don't you?

PAST SIMPLE

did

He left, didn't he?

FUTURE

will

She will come, won't she?

PERFECT

has / have / had

They have gone, haven't they?

MODALS

same modal

You can swim, can't you?

CONTINUOUS

is / are / was

He is reading, isn't he?

Flip the sign, copy the helping verb, swap the noun for a pronoun. Ram can swim, can't he?

2.2  Rules that win marks

I AM ...

aren't I

I am right, aren't I?

LET US / LET'S

shall we

Let us go, shall we?

COMMANDS

will you

Close the door, will you?

NEGATIVE COMMAND

will you

Don't go, will you?

NOBODY, NO ONE, NONE

positive tag with they

Nobody came, did they?

EVERYONE, SOMEONE

tag with they

Everyone knows, don't they?

SELDOM, HARDLY, NEVER

count as negative → positive tag

He never lies, does he?

THERE IS / THERE ARE

tag keeps there

There is a pen, isn't there?

THIS / THAT

tag with it

This is easy, isn't it?

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Never use a noun in the tag: Ram is kind, isn't Ram → isn't he.

 Hardly / seldom / never make the statement negative: He hardly speaks, doesn't he → does he.

 I am takes aren't I, not amn't I.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     N E G A T I V E   W O R D S   F L I P   T O O

Words like never, seldom, rarely, hardly, nobody, nothing are negative. The tag is therefore positive: She seldom comes, does she?

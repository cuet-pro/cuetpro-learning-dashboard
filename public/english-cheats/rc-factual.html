Facts & Data

+ 6 question types

A factual passage informs: science, environment, economy, history. It has facts, figures and logical flow. The answers are in the passage, so match, do not guess.

Read the Passage Smartly

Structure first, details later

Start here: How to read a factual passage (follow the arrows)

Skim for structure, then read questions, then hunt the answers.

1.1  How a factual passage is built

TOPIC SENTENCE

first line of a paragraph states the idea

Water pollution is rising.

SUPPORT

facts, figures, examples

In 2020, 40% of rivers...

CONCLUSION

last lines summarise or advise

Therefore, we must...

1.2  Signal words to watch

CONTRAST

however, but, although, yet

the idea turns

CAUSE / RESULT

because, so, therefore, as a result

reasons and effects

ADDITION / EXAMPLE

moreover, for instance, such as

more detail

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     T O P - D O W N   R E A D I N G

First line, last line, every paragraph. Then read the questions. Then find the lines that answer them. You rarely need to read word by word.

Question Types and Attack Plan

Six types and how to beat each

2.1  Which question type?  (follow the arrows)

Name the question type, then use its method.

2.2  The six types

FACT

answer is stated

Find the line, match the words.

INFERENCE

answer is implied

Choose the safest conclusion.

MAIN IDEA

what the whole passage says

Use topic sentences.

VOCABULARY

meaning in context

Substitute and test.

TONE / PURPOSE

writer's attitude

objective, critical, hopeful.

TRUE / FALSE

one option is a distortion

Check each statement.

2.3  Worked mini-example

Passage: "Plastic takes up to 500 years to break down. Each year, about 8 million tonnes enter the oceans. However, recycling can cut this waste."

Q: HOW LONG DOES PLASTIC TAKE TO BREAK DOWN?

Fact. The passage says up to 500 years.

Q: WHAT IS THE WRITER'S TONE?

Tone. Facts first, then "however, recycling" = concerned but hopeful.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Extreme words (always, never, only, all) are usually wrong.

 Right words, wrong idea: an option may reuse passage words but change the meaning.

 Outside knowledge: answer only from the passage.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     F I N D ,   M A T C H ,   C H E C K

Find the line, match the option to it, check that no word is stronger or different.

3.3  Mixed practice: strategy questions

1.  A question starts "According to the passage..." Which type is it?

2.  An option says "always". What should you suspect?

3.  You must pick the best title. What should it cover?

4.  A word is asked "as used in the passage". What do you do?

5.  An option uses facts not in the passage. Keep or eliminate?

6.  Which part of a paragraph holds its main idea?

7.  What does "however" signal?

8.  "Which can be inferred?" asks for stated or implied?

9.  Should you answer from your own knowledge?

10.  Should you read the questions before or after skimming?

Answers: 1 fact, 2 it is too extreme, 3 the whole passage, 4 reread and substitute, 5 eliminate, 6 the first / last line, 7 a contrast, 8 implied, 9 no, 10 after skimming (then read questions, then hunt).

Singular / Plural

+ 12 rules

The verb must match its subject in number. Find the real subject first. Words in between do not count.

1

The Basic Match

Find the real subject, then match the verb

Start here: Which verb? (follow the arrows)

Subject first, verb second. Never match the verb to the nearest noun by habit.

1.1  Singular or plural?

SINGULAR SUBJECT

takes is / was / has / V+s

The teacher is kind.

PLURAL SUBJECT

takes are / were / have / V

The teachers are kind.

A AND B

two persons = plural

Tea and coffee are ready.

ONE IDEA WITH AND

one thing = singular

Bread and butter is my breakfast.

OR / NOR

verb agrees with the subject NEAREST to it

Either you or he is wrong.

ALONG WITH, AS WELL AS, WITH

ignore it; verb agrees with the first subject

The captain, along with the team, is here.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     C R O S S   O U T   T H E   E X T R A S

Cross out everything between the subject and the verb. The bouquet (of red roses) is lovely. Cross out "of red roses" and the verb is obvious.

2.2  More rules to remember

A NUMBER OF / THE NUMBER OF

a number of = plural, the number of = singular

A number of students are absent. The number of students is small.

ONE OF THE + PLURAL NOUN

verb is singular

One of my friends is a doctor.

EACH / EVERY

always singular

Every boy and girl has a book.

COLLECTIVE NOUNS

one unit = singular; members acting separately = plural

The jury has given its verdict. The jury are divided.

MANY A

singular

Many a man has failed.

THERE IS / ARE

verb matches the noun AFTER it

There are five boys. There is a pen.

WHO / THAT / WHICH

verb matches the noun before it

He is one of the boys who are late.

PAIR WORDS

scissors, trousers, spectacles: plural, but "a pair of" is singular

A pair of shoes is here.

UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS

furniture, advice, luggage, information: singular

The furniture is new.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

In one of the ... who sentences the verb is plural: He is one of the students who have passed. But the only one who is singular: He is the only one who has passed.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     N E W S   =   N O   P L U R A L

News, mathematics, physics, economics, politics, measles: they end in -s but are singular. No news is good news.

3.3  Mixed practice: choose the right verb

1.  The quality of the mangoes (is / are) poor.

2.  Each of the girls (has / have) a prize.

3.  Neither the teacher nor the students (was / were) present.

4.  Economics (is / are) an interesting subject.

5.  A number of books (is / are) missing.

6.  The number of books (is / are) small.

7.  Bread and butter (is / are) my usual breakfast.

8.  One of the boys (is / are) absent.

9.  The poor (needs / need) our help.

10.  There (is / are) many reasons for this.

Answers: 1 is, 2 has, 3 were, 4 is, 5 are, 6 is, 7 is, 8 is, 9 need, 10 are.

6 0 - S E C O N D   R E C A P

Find the real subject, ignore of / with / along with.

 And = plural, or / nor = nearest noun.

 Each, every, either, neither, one of = singular.

 News, maths, economics = singular; police, people, cattle = plural.

 A number of = plural, the number of = singular.

 There is / are follows the noun after it.

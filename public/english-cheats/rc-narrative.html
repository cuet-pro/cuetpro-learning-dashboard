Story & Character

+ 5 clue types

A narrative passage tells a story: events, characters, setting and feeling. Questions ask who, why, how they felt and what happens next. Follow the events and the emotions.

MEMORY HACK  |  CHARACTER = ACTION + SPEECH

A character's trait is shown by what they do and say, not only what the writer calls them. "She ran back to help" = brave and kind.

2.3  Worked mini-example

Passage: "Meena's hands shook as she opened the envelope. She read the first line, then burst into tears of joy. She had passed."

Q: HOW DID MEENA FEEL AT FIRST?

Emotion. "hands shook" = nervous.

Q: WHY DID SHE CRY?

Motive. "tears of joy... passed" = relief and happiness.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Reading too much in: choose what the text shows, not what you imagine.

 Opposite feelings: "tears" can mean joy or sorrow; check the next words.

 Sequence: use words like first, then, finally.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     B O D Y   S I G N A L S

Shaking hands = nervous. Smiling = pleased. Looking away = shy or guilty. Clenched fists = angry.

3.3  Mixed practice: strategy questions

1.  "Her hands shook." What does it show?

2.  How is a character's trait best judged?

3.  "Why did he leave?" Where do you look?

4.  "What happens next?" What do you use?

5.  What is the theme of a story?

6.  Smiling and humming shows which feeling?

7.  "First... Then... Finally" helps with which question?

8.  Can you assume facts not in the passage?

9.  What is "setting"?

10.  What shows mood best?

Answers: 1 nervousness, 2 actions and speech, 3 just before the action, 4 the last clues, 5 the lesson or big idea, 6 joy, 7 sequence, 8 no, 9 time and place, 10 adjectives and verbs.

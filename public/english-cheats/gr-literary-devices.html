Figures of Speech

Literary devices are tools writers use to make language vivid. CUET asks you to name the device in a line. Learn the clue words and you can identify almost any device.

1

Comparison Devices

Simile, metaphor, personification and more

Start here: Which device? (follow the arrows)

Look for like / as first, then for "is", then for human actions.

1.1  Comparison devices

SIMILE

compare with like / as

as cold as ice, like a rose

METAPHOR

direct comparison

Life is a journey.

PERSONIFICATION

human traits for non-human

The sun smiled on us.

HYPERBOLE

extreme exaggeration

I have told you a million times.

ONOMATOPOEIA

sound words

buzz, hiss, bang, splash

APOSTROPHE

talking to an absent thing

O Death, where is thy sting?

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     L I K E   A S   =   S I M I L E

See like or as between two things? Simile. No like / as, just "is"? Metaphor. A thing doing human actions? Personification.

2

Sound and Meaning Devices

Alliteration, irony, oxymoron and more

2.1  Which sound or meaning device?  (follow the arrows)

Sound repeated = alliteration. Opposite words = oxymoron. Opposite meaning = irony.

2.2  More devices

ALLITERATION

same starting sound

Sally sells seashells.

ASSONANCE

same vowel sound

The rain in Spain.

OXYMORON

two opposites together

Living death, cruel kindness

IRONY

opposite of what is expected

A pilot afraid of heights.

PARADOX

seems false but true

Less is more.

EUPHEMISM

soft word for harsh one

passed away (died)

PUN

play on a word

"I used to be a banker, but I lost interest."

ANTITHESIS

contrasting ideas in balance

To err is human, to forgive divine.

ANAPHORA

same word starts lines

We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight in the fields.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Simile vs metaphor: like / as = simile. "He is as brave as a lion" is a metaphor.

 Oxymoron vs paradox: oxymoron is two words (deafening silence); paradox is a whole statement.

 Irony vs sarcasm: sarcasm is spoken and sharp; irony is a situation or words.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     O X Y - M O R O N   =   S M A R T   F O O L

Oxymoron = a "sharp" and "foolish" word together. Opposite words side by side: a wise fool, bitter sweet, open secret.

3.3  Mixed practice: name the device

1.  The wind howled angrily.

2.  He is as strong as an ox.

3.  The classroom was a zoo.

4.  Peter picked a pickled pepper.

5.  A pilot afraid of flying.

6.  I have a million things to do.

7.  The thunder boomed.

8.  It was a bittersweet farewell.

9.  Less is more.

10.  He passed away peacefully.

Answers: 1 personification, 2 simile, 3 metaphor, 4 alliteration, 5 irony, 6 hyperbole, 7 onomatopoeia, 8 oxymoron, 9 paradox, 10 euphemism.

6 0 - S E C O N D   R E C A P

Simile = like / as; metaphor = is.

 Personification = human actions for things.

 Hyperbole = exaggeration.

 Alliteration = same starting sound.

 Oxymoron = two opposite words.

 Irony = opposite of expected.

 Paradox = seems false, is true.

 Euphemism = soft word.

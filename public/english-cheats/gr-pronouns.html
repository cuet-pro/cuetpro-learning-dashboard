Case & Agreement

+ who / whom

A pronoun takes the place of a noun. CUET asks about case (I or me), agreement with the noun, and who / whom, each other.

1

Types and Case

Subject, object and possessive forms

Start here: I or me? (follow the arrows)

Doer = subject form, receiver = object form.

1.1  Pronoun forms

I

me, my, mine

I like it.

YOU

you, your, yours

You are kind.

HE / SHE

him / her

He met her.

WE

us, our, ours

We saw them.

THEY

them, their, theirs

They helped us.

IT

it, its

Its tail is long.

WHO

whom, whose

Whom did you meet?

REFLEXIVE

myself, himself, themselves

She hurt herself.

1.2  Joined pronouns and "between"

BETWEEN / EXCEPT / BUT + PRONOUN

always object form

Between you and me. Nobody but him came.

THAN / AS (COMPARISON)

finish the sentence in your head

She is taller than I (am).

ORDER OF PRONOUNS

In good: 2nd, 3rd, 1st; in bad: 1st first

You, he and I won. I and he were wrong.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     D R O P   T H E   O T H E R   P E R S O N

Cross out the extra name. Ravi and (I / me) went. → "I went", so Ravi and I went. She gave it to Ravi and (I / me). → "to me", so Ravi and me.

2

Agreement and Special Pronouns

Matching the noun, plus who, whom, each other

2.1  Which pronoun fits?  (follow the arrows)

The pronoun must match its noun in person and number.

2.2  Rules that win marks

WHO / WHOM

who = subject, whom = object

Who called? Whom did you call?

EACH OTHER / ONE ANOTHER

each other = two, one another = more

Ram and Sita love each other.

REFLEXIVE

only when the subject acts on itself

He hurt himself. Not: Myself went

EITHER, NEITHER, EACH, EVERYONE

singular, so use his / her

Everyone must bring his or her book.

POSSESSIVE NO APOSTROPHE

its, yours, hers, ours, theirs

The dog wagged its tail.

RELATIVE PRONOUNS

who = person, which = thing, that = both

The man who came is my uncle.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Its (belongs to it) has no apostrophe; it's means "it is": The dog wagged it's tail → its.

 Whom follows a preposition: To who → To whom.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     W H O   O R   W H O M

Try he / him. If "he" fits, use who; if "him" fits, use whom. (Who / Whom) did you meet? → "You met him" → whom.

3

Traps, Practice and Recap

The mistakes examiners love, then test yourself

3.1  Who, whom or whose?  (follow the arrows)

Substitute he or him to decide.

3.2  Favourite exam traps

WRONG

RIGHT

WHY

Between you and I

Between you and me

between + object

She is taller than me

She is taller than I

than I (am)

The dog wagged it's tail

The dog wagged its tail

possessive: no apostrophe

3.3  Mixed practice: choose the right pronoun

1.  Between you and (I / me), this is a secret.

2.  (Who / Whom) did you invite?

3.  She is as tall as (I / me).

4.  The dog wagged (its / it's) tail.

5.  He hurt (him / himself) while cooking.

6.  Ram and (I / me) are friends.

7.  The man (who / which) called is my uncle.

8.  Each boy must bring (his / their) own pen.

9.  This book is (my / mine).

10.  Ravi and Sita like (each other / one another).

Answers: 1 me, 2 Whom, 3 I, 4 its, 5 himself, 6 I, 7 who, 8 his, 9 mine, 10 each other.

Subject form before the verb, object form after verb / preposition.

 Between, except, but + object.

 Than / as: finish the sentence in your head.

 Who = he, whom = him.

 Its has no apostrophe.

 Each, either, neither, everyone = singular.

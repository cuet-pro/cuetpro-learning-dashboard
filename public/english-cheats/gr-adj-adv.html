An adjective describes a noun; an adverb describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb. CUET tests which to choose, degrees of comparison, and word order.

1

Adjective or Adverb?

Which word to use and how to tell

Start here: Adjective or adverb? (follow the arrows)

Adjectives describe things, adverbs describe actions.

1.1  Common patterns

ADJECTIVE + -LY

makes an adverb

quick → quickly, careful → carefully

SAME FORM BOTH WAYS

fast, hard, late, early, daily

He runs fast. A fast train.

DIFFERENT MEANING

hard / hardly, late / lately, near / nearly

He works hard. He hardly works.

LINKING VERBS

be, seem, look, feel, taste, sound take adjectives

She looks pale.

GOOD / WELL

good = adjective, well = adverb

She sings well. It is a good song.

ENOUGH

after adjective / adverb, before noun

tall enough, enough time

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     H A R D   V S   H A R D L Y

hard = with effort, hardly = almost not. He works hard. / He hardly works. Likewise late = not on time, lately = recently.

2

Degrees and Order

Positive, comparative, superlative and word order

2.1  Which degree?  (follow the arrows)

Two = -er and than. Three or more = the -est.

2.2  Rules that win marks

SHORT WORDS

-er / -est

tall, taller, tallest

LONG WORDS

more / most

beautiful, more beautiful, most beautiful

IRREGULAR

good-better-best, bad-worse-worst, many-more-most

better, worse

LATIN COMPARATIVES

senior, junior, superior, inferior take TO not than

He is senior to me.

ELDER / OLDER

elder = family only

my elder brother; older than me

FARTHER / FURTHER

farther = distance, further = more

farther away, further details

ORDER OF ADJECTIVES

opinion, size, age, colour, origin, material

a lovely small old red Indian wooden box

DOUBLE COMPARISON

never more + -er

more taller → taller

ABSOLUTE ADJECTIVES

unique, perfect, complete: no degrees

more perfect

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Comparative + than, superlative + the: She is more smarter than me → She is smarter than me.

 He is the more tall → He is taller.

 Fewer for countable, less for uncountable: fewer pens, less water.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     O S A S C O M P

Adjective order: Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. a beautiful old Chinese silk dress.

Traps, Practice and Recap

The mistakes examiners love, then test yourself

3.1  Few, a few, little, a little?  (follow the arrows)

a few / a little = some. few / little = hardly any.

3.2  Favourite exam traps

WRONG

RIGHT

WHY

3.3  Mixed practice: choose the correct word

1.  She dances (beautiful / beautifully).

2.  The flowers smell (sweet / sweetly).

3.  He is (more clever / cleverer) than his brother.

4.  This is the (best / better) film of the year.

5.  She is junior (than / to) me.

6.  He (hard / hardly) ever visits us.

7.  There is (few / little) milk left.

8.  (Fewer / Less) people attended.

9.  Ravi is the (taller / tallest) of the three.

10.  She speaks (good / well).

Answers: 1 beautifully, 2 sweet, 3 cleverer, 4 best, 5 to, 6 hardly, 7 little, 8 Fewer, 9 tallest, 10 well.

6 0 - S E C O N D   R E C A P

Adjective = noun, adverb = verb / adjective.

 Linking verbs (look, feel, taste) + adjective.

 Good = adjective, well = adverb.

 Two things = -er / more + than; three or more = the -est / most.

 Senior, junior, prefer + to.

 Fewer = countable, less = uncountable.

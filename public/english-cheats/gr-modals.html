Can / May / Must

+ perfect modals

Modals (can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, need, dare, ought to) show ability, permission, duty, advice and possibility. A modal is always followed by the base verb (V1).

1

What Each Modal Means

Ability, permission, duty and possibility

Name the idea first, then pick the modal.

1.1  Modals at a glance

CAN

ability, informal permission

She can speak French.

COULD

past ability, polite request

Could you help me?

MAY

permission, possibility

May I borrow your pen?

MIGHT

weak possibility

He might be late.

MUST

duty, strong certainty

He must be at home.

SHOULD

advice, duty

You should work harder.

WILL

future, promise

I will help you.

WOULD

past habit, polite

He would visit us every summer.

SHALL

I / we questions, offers

Shall we begin?

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     M O D A L   +   V 1 ,   A L W A Y S

A modal is followed by the base verb, with no -s, -ed or -ing. She can sings → She can sing. He must goes → He must go.

2

Rules and Perfect Modals

Past forms and the patterns CUET repeats

2.1  Modal for the past?  (follow the arrows)

For the past: modal + have + V3.

2.2  Rules that win marks

MUST VS HAVE TO

must = personal; have to = outside rule

I must study. I have to wear a uniform.

MUSTN'T VS NEEDN'T

mustn't = forbidden; needn't = not necessary

You mustn't smoke. You needn't come.

USED TO

past habit that stopped

I used to play cricket.

NEED / DARE

modal in negatives and questions

Need I go? He dare not speak.

OUGHT TO

always with to

You ought to obey.

WOULD RATHER

preference, then V1

I would rather stay.

HAD BETTER

strong advice, then V1

You had better leave now.

CAN / MAY

can = ability; may = permission

May I go out?

NO DOUBLE MODALS

only one modal in a clause

She will can come → She will be able to come.

E X A M   T R A P S   W I T H

Two modals together are wrong: You should must go.

 Ought needs to: You ought obey → You ought to obey.

 Mustn't and needn't are not the same.

M E M O R Y   H A C K     |     S H O U L D   H A V E   =   R E G R E T

should have + V3 = a past mistake. must have + V3 = a past guess you are sure about. You should have studied. / He must have forgotten.

3.3  Mixed practice: choose the right modal

1.  (May / Can) I use your phone? (polite request)

2.  He (must / might) be at home; the lights are on.

3.  You (should have / will have) listened to me.

4.  You (mustn't / needn't) hurry; there is plenty of time.

5.  She can (swim / swims) very well.

6.  You (ought / ought to) respect elders.

7.  You (had better / had) leave now.

8.  I (would rather / will rather) stay home.

9.  He (used to / use to) play football.

10.  (Could / Must) you pass the salt, please?

Answers: 1 May, 2 must, 3 should have, 4 needn't, 5 swim, 6 ought to, 7 had better, 8 would rather, 9 used to, 10 Could.

6 0 - S E C O N D   R E C A P

Modal + V1 always.

 Can = ability; may = permission; must = duty; should = advice.

 Mustn't = forbidden; needn't = not necessary.

 Past: should have / must have / may have + V3.

 Ought to, used to, would rather, had better + V1.

 One modal per clause.
